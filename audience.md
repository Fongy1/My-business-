# audience.md — Tradie Fight Fit

## Who they are

MMA fighters trying to take it to the next level while working a normal 9-5 or trade job. Trying to figure out how to schedule and track training to optimise it, and how to find time for extra training and recovery around a full work week.

## Problem you solve

Finding time to train and recover around work.

## Offer

Not locked in yet. Working concept, pulled from the design system pass: weight-cut tracker + sparring journal + motivation feed, no-code build (Adalo). Pricing tiers mocked so far — Free / Fighter ($9.99/mo) / Camp ($79/yr) — price still open, not committed.

## Where they hang out

Reddit, Instagram, maybe TikTok and YouTube.

## Research: real language, r/MMA_Academy

Source: two threads in r/MMA_Academy — "what's your day job" style thread (24-25d old) and an older thread about fitting in training hours (243d old, top comment at 46 upvotes). Quotes below are verbatim from the screenshots, usernames included so this stays traceable back to real people, not invented personas.

### How they describe their situation — always job + exact hours, no fluff

> "Construction 7am-3pm, train from 5:30pm-8pm" — NextAccess6482

> "I work 60 hours a week. I just got in the morning one hour" — surpsurf

> "Construction Sales, basically make my own hours but typically from 6am- 1:30-2pm mostly everyday." — LT81

> "I'm personally in IT, working a 7:30-4." — Born_to_Suffer_

This is the pattern: nobody describes their situation in feelings first — they lead with job + shift block. Any copy or onboarding flow should let them self-identify the same way (job type + hours), not with a vague "lifestyle" question.

### They self-identify as blue collar — use this word

> "I feel, I met more blue collar guys than anything else. In my boxing gym, we have a good amount of cops but I'm personally in IT... I personally think 9-5 are great for a lot of fighters if the pay is good." — Born_to_Suffer_ (19 upvotes)

Direct validation for the "tradie" framing — the audience already uses "blue collar" to describe itself in this exact context.

### Pain point #1 (highest engagement in the whole thread — 46 upvotes): family time vs. training time, not work vs. training

> "I have two kids under 5 and an office job. At this point in my life if I can get 2 nights a week that's a great week. Telling your wife you can't help with bedtime because you have an adult karate class rarely goes over well." — Pudge223 (46 upvotes)

The top comment in the older thread isn't about work hours at all — it's about the guilt/conflict of training time competing with family time. This is a stronger pain point than "my job is exhausting" and should not be ignored just because the current framing (tradie/shift-worker) leans job-first.

### Pain point #2: early-morning training creates work anxiety, not fitness gains

> "Used to train in morning than at night, 3-4 years ago- but gave me way too much anxiety with work and always had to play 'catch up'. For me it's absolutely perfect for work, life, training." — LT81

"Catch up" is the exact word for the fear here — training doesn't just cost time, it creates a debt at work that has to be repaid. A scheduling tool that reduces that "catch up" feeling (not just logs sessions) is the actual promise.

### The escape-hatch fantasy — recurring, not a one-off

> "Hi, gainfully employed PoW (person of workforce) here looking to get more training time - How do I become unemployed? Do you think it's attainable for me? I really feel like my true career is being a below average recreational athlete but don't know how to make the pivot :(" — Select-Bee-2166

> "the amount of people at my gym that are unemployed and train everyday (but they don't compete) is ridiculously high." — iranoutofideasz

> "We all need to find a husband with 3 income streams or 3 separate husbands" — ballbreak1

Made as jokes, but the joke is the tell: the fantasy isn't "a better schedule," it's "no job at all." Nobody in this thread believes that's realistic — which is exactly why a tool that makes the job-and-training reality less painful (rather than promising to escape it) is credible to them.

### Dream state: what "solved" looks like

> "My solution to that is for my 7yo daughter to train with me. Her jiu jitsu classes mostly overlap my MT training so we can both be at the gym together. I usually get in 10-12 hours of training per week." — marcomauythai (15 upvotes)

> "2-2.5 hours every evening Mon-Fri, and then [weekends]" — marcomauythai, replying to "10-12 hours a week? How?"

The people who've "solved it" didn't get more free time — they got resourceful about stacking training into existing life structure. That's the emotional win state to sell: not "we'll give you more hours," but "here's how to make the hours you have work without the anxiety or the guilt."

## Synthesis

- Lead with job + hours, not vibes — that's literally how they introduce themselves.
- "Blue collar" is a word this audience already uses about itself — safe to use in copy.
- The sharpest pain isn't work fatigue, it's the anxiety of falling behind at work ("catch up") and the guilt of training time vs. family time. Both outrank "I'm tired" as the real problem.
- Don't sell escape from the job — sell relief from the catch-up spiral and the guilt, while keeping the job. The audience has already made peace with keeping the job; the fantasy of quitting is a joke, not a real want.
- Next concrete action: run these same two searches on Instagram/TikTok comments (not posts) to see if "catch up" and "blue collar" show up outside Reddit too — one confirming data point from a second platform is enough to lock this language in before writing app copy.
